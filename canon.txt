HOW TO USE THIS FILE
======================================================================

This file combines the useful lore, character, visual, relationship, academy,
map, and game-design information found across the repository.

STATUS LABELS

LOCKED
Directly described as canon, fixed, non-negotiable, absolute, or repeatedly
supported by multiple files.

ESTABLISHED
Clearly present in a dedicated source file, but not explicitly labelled locked.

IMPLEMENTED
Present in the current playable web prototype. This may represent intended
canon, but implementation alone does not make it permanent story canon.

OPTIONAL / ROUTE DESIGN
A possible romance, ending, player route, or dramatic setup. It is valid design
material, not a confirmed event in the story.

DRAFT / NEEDS CONFIRMATION
Present only in an older prototype, concept image, inconsistent file, or
conflicting source.

UNKNOWN
Not established by the repository.

IMPORTANT COVERAGE NOTE

The repository contains character bibles, relationship systems, Orders,
visual references, a fantasy-Australia concept map, web-game prototypes,
3D character assets, and a starter Unity project.

The ZIP does not contain the full Tidefall book manuscripts or a complete
book-by-book plot outline. This file therefore records the canon available in
the library, but it cannot reconstruct plot events that are not stored there.

======================================================================
1. CORE IDENTITY
======================================================================

TITLE / WORLD NAME
Tidefall

PRIMARY SETTING
Tidefall Academy and Tidefall Castle, a coastal Australian magic academy.

CORE CULTURAL ANCHOR
Australia is not decorative background. Climate, beaches, coastal life,
casual clothing, and the contrast between normal Australian life and magical
danger are part of Tidefall's identity.

CURRENT CENTRAL CAST
LOCKED: The main trio is Jasper Vale, Harper Vale, and Ava Monroe.

DEVELOPMENT NOTE OUTSIDE THE REPOSITORY
Lily is a possible fourth main character who may be introduced in Book 4.
She is not currently an established member of the trio and is not present in
the uploaded repository.

CENTRAL NARRATIVE ENGINE
The trio embodies three different responses to pressure:

Jasper chooses.
Harper forces action by making the immediate danger impossible to ignore.
Ava exposes the delayed cost and long-term consequences.

The story works best when none of them has a perfect answer.

2. THE MAIN TRIO
======================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------
JASPER VALE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

STATUS
LOCKED main character.
Player character and narrative centre in the game design.
Primary duelist.
First appears at the school gates.

CORE THEME
Choice under pressure.
The cost of choosing.
He matters whether he wants to or not.

CORE FUNCTION
Jasper is the decision axis of the trio. He carries moral weight, receives
incomplete information, and must choose between outcomes that all have costs.
When the trio disagrees, Jasper chooses. He must not hand responsibility to
someone else merely to avoid blame.

PERSONALITY
Thoughtful, cautious but not cowardly, empathetic, observant, self-questioning,
and capable under pressure. He listens before acting. He notices emotional
changes in other people. He often hesitates before speaking or choosing, but
once committed he follows through completely.

Jasper takes responsibility even when an event is not entirely his fault.
He second-guesses successful outcomes and tends to carry problems alone.
He rarely boasts and is not an action-first show-off.

He never abandons someone during a crisis, blames others to escape guilt, says
that a crisis is not his problem, or casually assumes someone else will handle
it.

BACKSTORY PRESSURE
Jasper grew up knowing he was capable without understanding what that capability
meant. Adults quietly expected him to handle difficult situations. Early events
reinforced a dangerous belief: when he acted, things improved; when he
hesitated, consequences followed.

Another design document describes the unseen event that broke him:
he made a careful, morally right choice and harm still followed. This proved
that thinking carefully does not guarantee safety. He internalised
responsibility for consequences.

TRAUMATIC PRESSURE POINTS
- Being present when something went wrong and believing he should have stopped it.
- Being praised for surviving rather than for choosing.
- Realising that winning a duel does not prevent loss elsewhere.
- Being forced to choose between people he cares about.
- Having his hesitation publicly exposed.
- Being told his potential matters more than his judgement.

DEEPEST FEAR
Choosing wrong again, acting without certainty, or making a decision alone.

WHAT HE AVOIDS
The possibility that some outcomes are unavoidable. He resists fate and
prophecy because accepting inevitability could become an excuse for inaction.

EMOTIONAL BEHAVIOUR
Jasper carries guilt quietly. He replays decisions, seeks confirmation, delays
commitment, avoids eye contact, goes still, and answers briefly. His voice
steadies in combat but can waver in personal moments. He minimises his own pain.

ATTACHMENT AND TRUST
Trust must be earned. Once earned, Jasper's loyalty is deep and stubborn.
When detaching, he withdraws instead of confronting immediately.

He feels safest when people are honest during conflict and when he is allowed
to fail without being judged as worthless.

Jealousy or trust pressure can be triggered when he is excluded from major
decisions or when another person assumes responsibility for his mistake.

DIALOGUE
Short to medium sentences. Clear and direct. Rarely rambles.
Measured when calm. Clipped and controlled when angry. Understated when hurt.
Hard truths are often preceded by a pause.

He would not say:
"It's not my problem."
"Someone else can handle it."
"I didn't care anyway."

VISUAL IDENTITY
Upright, alert, never completely relaxed. Weight slightly forward.
Eyes scan his surroundings. His grip tightens before combat.
Practical clothing rather than flair.
Magic is clean and controlled, not wild or showy.

VISUAL CONFLICT TO RESOLVE
The group art, Jasper turn-around image, and current web prototype depict Jasper
with blond hair. Most numbered Jasper style sheets depict him with black hair.
Blond currently has stronger cross-source support, but this must be formally
confirmed before treating hair colour as locked.

GAMEPLAY IDENTITY
Momentum-based dueling.
Precision over raw power.
Adaptive spell use.
High trust causes allies to respond faster and unlocks combinations.
Low trust makes allies hesitate.
Broken trust creates defensive penalties and temporarily narrows dialogue.

PLAYER RESPONSE LOGIC
- Hesitation increases world tension and may cause NPCs to act independently.
- Recklessness can create immediate success and long-term damage.
- Lies create guilt and return later.
- Unfair victories still count as victories, but relationships strain.
The method of winning matters.

ARC
Early: reactive, capable but unsure, afraid of choosing incorrectly.
Middle: proactive, owns outcomes, clashes with protectors and planners.
Possible end states:
- A confident leader who accepts blame.
- An isolated protector carrying the cost alone.
- A balanced leader who trusts others with choice.

HARD WRITING LIMITS
Do not make Jasper a blank or silent player avatar.
Do not make him cruel, dismissive, reckless, instantly decisive, or permanently
confident.
Do not remove consequences from his choices.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
HARPER VALE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

STATUS
LOCKED main character.
Fixed companion.
Protector and moral enforcer.
Appears beside Jasper during the arrival sequence.

CORE THEME
Protection at any cost.
If Harper steps in, something is already wrong.

CORE FUNCTION
Harper represents the immediate cost. She asks who gets hurt now and what breaks
if the trio waits. She creates urgency through time limits, physical danger,
and emotional immediacy.

Harper is always present in the central design. Antagonists must not remove her
through routine kidnapping, incapacitation, unexplained absence, or a stronger
enemy created merely to sideline her. Instead, the conflict should make her
strength costly.

PERSONALITY
Solid, direct, capable, decisive, protective, and difficult to stop once danger
escalates. Harper does not wait for consensus during violence. She intervenes
because her experience taught her that someone else will wait too long.

She prioritises Jasper's safety over rules, challenges authority, makes
unilateral decisions, and frames action as necessity rather than preference.
She rarely asks for emotional support and rarely admits uncertainty aloud.

She never leaves someone defenceless, waits for permission during immediate
violence, manipulates people to get her way, or becomes passive.

BACKSTORY PRESSURE
Harper learned that hesitation gets people hurt. Moments in her past where she
did hesitate caused someone else to pay the cost. The narrative should neither
fully exonerate nor fully condemn her. Harper believes her interventions were
necessary even when they haunt her.

She does not regret acting.
She regrets acting alone.

TRAUMATIC PRESSURE POINTS
- Being the only person willing to step in.
- Having her actions criticised after they prevented a worse outcome.
- Being praised for strength but distrusted for judgement.
- Being told she made the situation worse.
- Being physically or institutionally prevented from acting during danger.
- Watching Jasper suffer because he hesitated.

DEEPEST FEAR
Powerlessness.

WHAT SHE AVOIDS
The possibility that force can become cruelty. She insists on a difference
between necessary and excessive action while privately fearing that she may no
longer know exactly where the line is.

EMOTIONAL BEHAVIOUR
Harper expresses loyalty through action. Under stress she becomes more
controlling rather than more distant. When hurt, she speaks less and becomes
hyper-efficient. She raises her voice mainly when ignored. Her strongest anger
is often quiet.

ATTACHMENT AND TRUST
Protective bonding. Direct confrontation is a safety signal. She needs to be
trusted with real responsibility.

Jealousy and trust pressure can be triggered when Jasper trusts someone else
with combat decisions or when authority figures reduce her role.

DIALOGUE
Short, plain, practical, and command-oriented under pressure.
Minimal metaphor. Emotion is implied instead of explained.
Calm Harper is blunt, watchful, and quietly reassuring.
Angry Harper accuses directly without hedging.

She would not say:
"Do whatever you want."
"I don't care."
"I was wrong to step in."
"Someone else will handle it."

VISUAL IDENTITY
Grounded and unyielding. Instinctively places herself between danger and others.
Minimal wasted movement. Steps forward as conflict rises.
Guarded and assessing expression.
Eye-line elevated and scanning.
Hands ready even while apparently relaxed.
Magic manifests forcefully and physically.

Harper must look capable. Framing or clothing should not imply helplessness or
indecision.

VISUAL IDENTIFIERS
The repository consistently depicts Harper with red or auburn hair, often long
or braided, and green eyes. Her clothing system moves between academy uniform,
modified uniform, sleepwear, casual Australian clothes, action clothing,
mourning, structured public clothing, formal dress, and beach/swimwear.

GAMEPLAY IDENTITY
Defensive anchor and threat controller.
High trust unlocks damage mitigation and emergency intervention.
Low trust causes unpredictable intervention and can temporarily override player
actions during crises.
Broken trust causes Harper to operate independently and removes coordinated
strategies.

Loss of trust never makes Harper powerless. It changes how she helps.

ARC
Early: absolute protector; control through force.
Middle: conflicted; struggles with restraint.
Possible end states:
- A balanced guardian who trusts others.
- An isolated sentinel carrying the cost alone.
- A loyal equal who protects without dominating.

HARD WRITING LIMITS
Do not make Harper passive, comic relief, incompetent, or merely a support
character.
Do not casually remove her from a problem.
Breaking a core rule must permanently alter her relationship with Jasper and
carry an irreversible cost.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
AVA MONROE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

STATUS
LOCKED main character.
Strategist, analyst, and long-view decision maker.
First appears early in an academic setting, observing before acting.

CORE THEME
Control through understanding.
She already knows what this will cost.

CORE FUNCTION
Ava represents delayed cost. She identifies patterns, predicts systemic damage,
tracks reputation and resource consequences, and warns what a choice will make
possible later.

Ava should speak less often than Harper. Her warnings are not tutorials.
Most choices receive no Ava warning. This preserves the weight of the moments
when she does speak.

PERSONALITY
Composed, intentional, precise, observant, and usually two steps ahead.
She evaluates risk before acting, tracks consequences internally, speaks late,
offers solutions more readily than comfort, and masks stress through efficiency.

Ava rarely acts emotionally in public or raises her voice. She never gambles
blindly, delegates critical decisions casually, or chooses comfortable
ignorance over uncertainty.

Ava must never feel random.

BACKSTORY PRESSURE
Ava learned that outcomes matter more than intentions. She became skilled at
recognising repeating conflicts, predicting collapse, and seeing regret before
others did. She was often right too early. People ignored her warnings, then the
events unfolded as predicted.

Ava does not fear being wrong.
She fears being right and ignored.

TRAUMATIC PRESSURE POINTS
- Watching preventable harm occur after she warned people.
- Being called cold for choosing the least-worst outcome.
- Being relied upon for answers she never wanted to give.
- Being forced to decide without enough information.
- Having impulsive action destroy a plan.
- Being told feelings matter more than results.

WHAT SHE AVOIDS
The fact that attachment can cloud judgement. She cares deeply about the trio
but maintains emotional distance because she fears hesitation at the decisive
moment.

HOW SHE BREAKS
Not through visible panic. Through overcorrection when control collapses.

ATTACHMENT AND TRUST
Trust grows gradually and usually begins with intellectual alignment.
When detaching, Ava becomes formal and emotionally distant.

She feels safest when she is taken seriously and her plans are respected even
when they are not followed.

Excluding her from planning or making major decisions without consulting her
damages trust.

DIALOGUE
Medium-length, precise sentences.
Analytical vocabulary with feeling hidden beneath neutral language.
When stressed, she may speak faster.
When overwhelmed, she becomes silent.
When angry, she is cold, controlled, and devastatingly direct.
When hurt, she becomes formal and avoids personal language.

She would not say:
"It doesn't matter."
"I didn't think about that."
"We'll deal with it later."
"I wasn't paying attention."

VISUAL IDENTITY
Composed posture, upright without stiffness.
Deliberate and economical movement.
Neutral, attentive expression.
Steady eye contact.
Hands still or occupied with notes or objects.
Orderly, intentional clothing.
Magic appears in clean lines with minimal excess.

Ava should not look chaotic, including under stress, until a deliberately
written breaking point.

VISUAL IDENTIFIERS
The visual assets consistently depict Ava with a short dark-brown or black bob
and green eyes. Her clothing system includes academy wear, formal looks, quiet
magic, casual clothes, action gear, mourning, sleepwear, beachwear, and
functional swimwear.

GAMEPLAY IDENTITY
Tactical enhancer and risk mitigator.
High trust unlocks predictive bonuses, fewer random negative outcomes, and
advanced planning options.
Low trust reduces foresight.
Broken trust means Ava withholds strategic input. She does not sabotage; she
stops compensating.

PLAYER RESPONSE LOGIC
- Hesitation: Ava offers analysis or probability framing.
- Recklessness: she warns once, then reduces support if the pattern continues.
- Lies: she records inconsistencies and trust erodes slowly.
- Unfair success: she acknowledges the result while identifying future cost.

ARC
Early: detached strategist who believes control prevents loss.
Middle: conflicted planner learning the value of human unpredictability.
Possible end states:
- A balanced tactician who trusts others.
- An isolated mind carrying silent guilt.
- A partner who chooses people over certainty.

HARD WRITING LIMITS
Do not make Ava impulsive.
Do not reduce her to exposition.
Do not make her cruel or dismissive of suffering.
Do not let every plan succeed without cost.
A core rule can break only when Ava consciously abandons control and is
permanently changed by the consequence.

3. THE TRIO AS A SYSTEM
======================================================================

LOCKED CONFLICT ROLES

Jasper: decision axis.
Harper: immediate cost.
Ava: delayed cost.

GOLDEN RULE
When all three disagree, Jasper chooses.

Harper forces urgency.
Ava defines consequences.
The disagreement is pressure around Jasper's decision, not a contest to seize
the protagonist role.

PRIMARY CONFLICT TYPES

1. Speed versus safety
Harper: act now or someone is harmed.
Ava: acting now creates a repeating future problem.
Jasper chooses between immediate loss and future instability.

2. Person versus system
Harper protects a specific person.
Ava protects the structure that keeps many people safe.
Jasper chooses who pays.

3. Risk versus control
Harper accepts a dangerous immediate route.
Ava proposes a controlled but slower or morally greyer route.
Jasper chooses between unpredictable danger and predictable compromise.

CONSEQUENCES OF IGNORING THEM

Ignore Harper:
Immediate physical consequences, injury, or preventable loss.

Ignore Ava:
Delayed betrayal, hostile systems, resource loss, route closures, or damaged
reputation.

Ignore both:
Chaos path, improvised survival, and fewer safety nets later.

DO NOT BREAK THE LANES

Do not have Harper and Ava unite merely to overrule Jasper.
Do not make Ava emotional simply to win an argument.
Do not suddenly turn Harper into the long-range strategist.
Do not let Jasper avoid responsibility by deferring the choice.

AVA WARNING SYSTEM

Soft warning:
Early story, optional, low consequence.

Firm warning:
Mid story, signals a lasting consequence.

Hard warning:
Late story, identifies permanence or route closure. Ava may later refuse to
help undo the choice.

Most decisions receive no warning.

HARPER PRESSURE SYSTEM

Harper creates short decision windows, escalating danger during hesitation and
clear immediate risk. She is what the player feels in the moment.

ANTAGONIST FOIL TYPES

The Escalator:
Creates repeated emergencies. Harper saves people each time, but every
intervention worsens the long-term situation.

The System Player:
Obeys rules while exploiting policy and precedent. Harper can act only by
sacrificing legitimacy. Ava must fight through systems.

The Pressure Mirror:
Protects their own people as fiercely as Harper does. Removes the trio's easy
moral high ground.

The Manipulator:
Hides behind people Harper must protect and uses her as a shield. Ava's options
collapse and Jasper is forced into ugly compromises.

The Chaos Variable:
Breaks patterns through emotional, irrational, or symbolic action. Harper can
respond, Ava cannot reliably predict, and Jasper must improvise.

CORE DESIGN ENGINE
Harper's strength creates a cost.
Ava sees the cost.
Jasper chooses who pays it.

ALARIC THORNE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Canonical spelling:
Alaric Thorne.

Repository variant:
The folder name says "throne". The dedicated file and relationship material use
Thorne, so Thorne is the preferred spelling.

Role:
Headmaster and strategic authority.

Core:
Experienced, politically aware, morally grey by necessity, and careful about
timing. He knows more than he says and says less than he knows.

Belief:
Truth sometimes must be delayed to prevent institutional collapse.
He has made compromised decisions from calculation rather than cruelty and does
not deny their harm.

Trio relationships:
- Jasper: potential and danger; a moral force who may act rightly at the wrong time.
- Harper: respected but feared for decisive action without restraint.
- Ava: intellectually trusted and sometimes given partial truths.

Gameplay:
Controls access to major plot information and resources.
Rewards restraint, judgement, and political awareness.

Dialogue:
Measured, layered, calm, often reframes the question.

Never:
Make him purely benevolent or secretly evil.

Romance-reaction doctrine:
Observes rather than immediately interferes. Acts when relationships destabilise
leadership, judgement, secrecy, or timing. He delays rather than simply forbids.

MARIBEL CROWE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Role:
Deputy Headmistress and institutional enforcer.

Structure given human form. Believes procedures and consequences protect

ROWAN HALE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Role:
Professor of Advanced Dueling.

Core:
Teaches combat brutally but not sadistically. Believes hesitant magic kills and
that clarity beats talent. Trains students for real violence without glorifying
it.

Trio relationships:
- Jasper: respected because he commits once engaged; pushed harder than others.
- Harper: respected as a natural combatant but challenged on instinct overriding strategy.

Gameplay:
Unlocks advanced dueling, risk-reward systems, and high-stakes challenges.

Dialogue:
Direct, cutting, instructional. Corrects failure instead of comforting it.

Dueling doctrine:
Wand up, intent clear. A bout ends at yield or disarm. The circle exists for
discipline, not spectacle.

Never:
Make him sadistic, comedic, or dismissive of survival.

Romance-reaction doctrine:
Indifferent unless combat performance declines. Uses harsh drills, separated
training, and public technical criticism to expose weakness.

7. THE ORDERS OF TIDEFALL
======================================================================

STATUS
LOCKED system.

Tidefall does not divide students by bloodline, gender, talent, or background.
Every student is inducted into a centuries-old academic and philosophical Order.

An Order is not a ranking of human worth.
It is a framework for pressure, mentorship, community, and growth.

WHAT AN ORDER DEFINES
- Mixed-gender dormitory wing, grouped by year.
- Primary mentors and tutors.
- Philosophical approach to magic.
- Internal expectations and discipline.
- Restricted spaces and Order-only routes.

Students from all Orders attend classes, train, and compete together.

Order changes are rare and discouraged.
Many important people eventually break from their Order's expected behaviour.

11. RELATIONSHIP SYSTEM
======================================================================

STATUS
Global relationship rules are labelled canon.

CORE PHILOSOPHY
Relationships are systems, not rewards.

- No relationship exists only to please the player.
- No relationship is guaranteed to succeed.
- Relationships remember behaviour.
- Strain, failure, toxicity, distance, repair, and changed forms are valid.
- Nothing resets automatically.

PRIMARY RELATIONSHIP TYPES
- Loyalty-based: Harper, Raf.
- Trust-based: Ava, Isla, Lachlan.
- Emotion-based: Mia, Benji.
- Morality-based: Keira, Talia.
- Transactional: Noah.
- Observational: Soren.

POSSIBLE STATES
Neutral, growing, stable, strained, toxic, broken, recovering.

Broken does not automatically mean ended.
Toxic does not mean a person is evil.
Recovery is possible but never free.

FAILURE CONDITIONS
- Repeating the same damaging choice.
- Apologies without changed action.
- Ignoring someone during crisis.
- Choosing outcomes over people when the specific character cannot accept that.
- Public silence or betrayal when public defence was needed.

FAILURE CHANGES THE RELATIONSHIP'S SHAPE
It does not automatically delete the character or make them a simple enemy.

RECOVERY REQUIRES ALL THREE
1. Acknowledgement of the actual action.
2. Cost, such as time, opportunity, power, or reputation.
3. Changed behaviour when the same pressure returns.

PUBLIC AND PRIVATE ACTIONS DIFFER
Public betrayal is not the same as private betrayal.
Public defence can matter more than private reassurance.
NPCs remember who saw what.

TOXICITY PATTERNS
- Harper: protection becomes control.
- Ava: logic replaces empathy.
- Raf: loyalty is taken for granted.
- Benji: fear governs decisions.
- Keira: morality becomes self-righteousness.

TEACHERS
Teachers do not participate romantically with students.
They may mentor, protect, oppose, restrict, investigate, or change access.
They react to effects, patterns, safety, discipline, performance, and ethics,
not to affection itself.

13. VISUAL AND STYLE CANON
======================================================================

PURPOSE
Styles communicate story, growth, emotion, culture, and context.
They are not random fashion changes.

CORE RULES
- A character keeps the same face, height, body type, and apparent age.
- Context changes clothing, not identity.
- Expressions must match the scene's emotional meaning.
- Clothing should be practical for location, activity, personality, and climate.
- Australia must remain visible in casual and coastal styling.
- Styles change due to story, place, time, and emotion, not randomness.

STYLE TYPES
Outfit styles define clothing.
Protective and action styles define state, posture, movement, and magic rather
than requiring a costume change.

THE 15-STYLE SYSTEM

1. Academy Uniform
Classes, exams, assemblies.
Regulation, structure, blending in.

2. Modified Uniform
After hours, restricted spaces, minor rule-breaking.
Subtle rebellion while remaining recognisably academy clothing.

3. Sneaking Out
Night exploration and curfew-breaking.
Thrown-together sleepwear plus practical outer layers.
Speed over appearance.

4. Dorm / Private Space
Safety, late-night conversation, and vulnerability.
Relaxed sleepwear.

5. Casual Day
Off-campus or free time.
Simple, intentionally normal Australian clothes.

6. Protective State
Danger anticipated.
Outfit remains; posture and positioning change.

7. Action / Crisis State
Danger happening now.
Current clothing may become damaged or messy.
Survival over control.

8. Mourning / After Loss
Dark colours, reduced effort, grief expressed without dialogue.

9. Structured / Composed
Public appearance after loss.
Clean lines and controlled presentation.
Holding it together rather than openly mourning.

10. Refined / Transitional
Growth between identities.
Layered, mature, intentional, but not fully settled.

11. Quiet Magic / Memory
Reflection, study, remembrance.
Subtle magic supports emotion instead of spectacle.

12. Confident / Self-Owned
Sharp, intentional self-knowledge.
No accidental sadness or defensiveness.

13. Formal / Ceremony
Rare balls, ceremonies, or public recognition.
Strength without armour.

14. Beach / Coastal Casual
Relaxed, sporty, Australian, barefoot or practical.
Freedom rather than glamour.

15. In Water
Functional swimwear and natural movement.
Realism over stylisation.

STYLE ARC
Act I: 1, 2, 5, 14.
Act II: 2, 3, 6.
Act III: 8, 7.
Act IV: 9, 10, 12.
Act V: 12, 13, 15.

CONTRAST RULE
High-impact scenes can deliberately place danger inside the wrong-looking
context:
formal clothing during action, dorm clothing during confrontation, or beach
clothing when devastating news arrives.

TRIO VISUAL GROWTH
Protector's styles change least.
Main decision-maker's styles change most.
Strategist's styles become increasingly practical.
Visual change should mirror emotional growth.

MAIN VISUAL REFERENCES

Harper:
