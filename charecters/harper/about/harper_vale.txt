CHARACTER NAME: Harper Vale
ROLE: Protector / Fixed Companion / Moral Enforcer
FIRST APPEARANCE: Arrival sequence, beside Jasper
CORE THEME: Protection at any cost
PLAYER READ: “If Harper steps in, something is already wrong.”
VISUAL IDENTITY (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
POSTURE:
- Solid, grounded, unyielding
- Stands between danger and others by instinct

MOVEMENT:
- Decisive, minimal wasted motion
- Steps forward, never back, when conflict escalates

DEFAULT EXPRESSION:
- Guarded
- Assessing
- Calm under threat, tense under emotional stress

OUTFITS COMMUNICATE:
- Readiness
- Control
- Authority without asking permission

VISUAL CONSTANTS:
- Eye-line always elevated, scanning
- Hands ready even when “relaxed”
- Magic manifests forcefully, with physical intent
Harper must always look capable.
Never dress or frame her in a way that suggests helplessness or indecision.

BACKSTORY
Harper learned early that hesitation gets people hurt.

Where Jasper internalised responsibility, Harper internalised intervention. She does not wait for consensus. She does not ask for approval. When something feels wrong, Harper acts — because in her experience, someone else always waits too long.

Her past includes moments where she did hesitate — and someone paid for it. The story should never fully exonerate or fully condemn her actions; Harper believes her choices were necessary, even if they haunt her.

Harper does not regret acting.
She regrets acting alone.

KEY TRAUMATIC PRESSURE POINTS
- Being the only one willing to step in
- Having her actions criticised after they prevent worse outcomes
- Being praised for strength but distrusted for judgement
WHAT HARPER AVOIDS THINKING ABOUT
Harper avoids the idea that force can become cruelty. She draws a hard line between “necessary” and “excessive” — and quietly fears she no longer knows where it lies.

WHAT BREAKS HARPER UNDER PRESSURE
Being told she made things worse

Being prevented from acting when danger is imminent

Watching Jasper suffer because he hesitated

Harper breaks not through fear, but through powerlessness.

PERSONALITY RULES (ABSOLUTE)
ALWAYS:
- Intervenes when danger escalates
- Prioritises Jasper’s safety over rules
- Speaks plainly in crisis

OFTEN:
- Challenges authority
- Makes unilateral decisions
- Frames actions as necessity, not choice

RARELY:
- Asks for emotional support
- Admits uncertainty aloud

NEVER:
- Leaves someone defenceless
- Waits for permission during violence
- Uses manipulation to get her way
These rules must not be bent casually.

RELATIONSHIP LOGIC
ATTACHMENT STYLE:
- Protective bonding
- Loyalty expressed through action

DETACHMENT STYLE:
- Emotional shutdown
- Increased control rather than withdrawal

JEALOUSY TRIGGERS:
- Jasper trusting someone else in combat decisions
- Authority figures restricting her role

SAFETY SIGNALS:
- Direct confrontation
- Being trusted with responsibility
ROMANCE RULES (HARPER IS NOT PASSIVE)
ROMANCE STARTS WHEN:
- Jasper proves he can choose under pressure
- Trust is mutual, not one-sided protection

ROMANCE DAMAGED WHEN:
- Jasper hides danger from her
- She is sidelined “for her own good”

TOXIC PHASE TRIGGERS:
- Harper overcontrolling Jasper’s choices
- Treating protection as ownership

RECOVERY CONDITIONS:
- Harper consciously choosing restraint
- Jasper asserting autonomy without rejecting her
Romance with Harper is intense, protective, volatile — and never soft by default.

DIALOGUE RULES (VERY IMPORTANT)
SENTENCE STRUCTURE:
- Short
- Direct
- Command-oriented under stress

VOCABULARY:
- Practical
- Minimal metaphor
- Emotion implied, not explained

EMOTIONAL TELLS:
- Raises voice only when ignored
- Quiet intensity when truly angry
WHEN CALM:
Blunt, watchful, quietly reassuring.

WHEN ANGRY:
Direct accusations. No hedging. No softening.

WHEN HURT:
Withdraws verbally, becomes hyper-efficient physically.

LINES HARPER MUST NEVER SAY:
“Do whatever you want.”

“I don’t care.”

“I was wrong to step in.”

“Someone else will handle it.”

GAMEPLAY MECHANICS TIED TO HARPER
MECHANICAL ROLE:
- Defensive anchor
- Threat controller
WHEN HARPER TRUST IS HIGH:
- Damage mitigation boosts
- Emergency interventions unlock

WHEN TRUST IS LOW:
- Harper intervenes unpredictably
- Can override player actions in crisis scenes

WHEN TRUST BREAKS:
- Harper acts independently
- Player loses access to coordinated strategies
Harper is never powerless.
Loss of trust changes how she helps — not whether she can.

PLAYER INTERACTION LOGIC (CRITICAL)
WHEN PLAYER HESITATES:
- Harper steps in
- Player agency narrows temporarily

WHEN PLAYER IS RECKLESS:
- Harper confronts immediately
- Protects others first, judges Jasper later

WHEN PLAYER LIES:
- Harper senses it
- Does not confront immediately
- Trust erosion is delayed but severe

WHEN PLAYER SUCCEEDS UNFAIRLY:
- Harper accepts results
- Challenges methods privately
Harper is not fooled easily — but she chooses when to act.

SCENE USAGE GUIDE (STRICT)
USE HARPER IN SCENES THAT:
- Escalate danger quickly
- Test leadership
- Force moral clarity

AVOID USING HARPER IN SCENES THAT:
- Require gentle ambiguity
- Remove immediate stakes

HARPER ELEVATES CONFLICT BY:
- Removing “safe” options
- Forcing action
- Making inaction visible
LONG-TERM ARC
EARLY HARPER:
- Absolute protector
- Control through force

MID-STORY HARPER:
- Conflicted
- Struggles with restraint

END-STATE POSSIBILITIES:
- Balanced guardian who trusts others
- Isolated sentinel who bears the cost alone
- Loyal equal who protects without dominating
HARD LIMITS (DO NOT BREAK)
NEVER:
- Make Harper passive
- Turn her into comic relief
- Remove her competence
- Reduce her to a “support character”

ALLOWED TO BREAK RULES IF:
- The moment permanently changes her relationship with Jasper
- The cost is irreversible
