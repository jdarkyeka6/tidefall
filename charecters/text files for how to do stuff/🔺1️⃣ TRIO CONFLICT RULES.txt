🔺 TRIO CONFLICT RULES

(What happens when Jasper, Harper, and Ava all disagree)

This is critical, because disagreement is where your best moments come from.

1️⃣ The Golden Rule (NEVER BREAK THIS)

When all three disagree:

Jasper chooses. Always.

But—

Harper forces urgency

Ava defines consequences

The conflict is pressure, not a power struggle.

2️⃣ Conflict Triangle Roles (LOCKED)

When a disagreement happens, each character MUST fall into one lane.

🧭 Jasper — The Decision Axis

Hears both sides

Has incomplete information

Choice defines tone, not just outcome

If Jasper hesitates too long:

Situation escalates

Choice becomes worse automatically

🛡️ Harper — The Immediate Cost

Harper asks:

“Who gets hurt now?”

“What breaks if we wait?”

She pressures Jasper with:

Time limits

Physical danger

Emotional immediacy

Harper does not care about future fallout in the moment.

🧠 Ava — The Delayed Cost

Ava asks:

“What does this unlock later?”

“What does this make inevitable?”

She pressures Jasper with:

Long-term reputation

Resource loss

Systemic collapse

Ava never rushes, even when danger is high.

3️⃣ Conflict Types (USE THESE)

Every trio disagreement must fit one of these types:

⚔️ Type A — Speed vs Safety

Harper: “If we don’t move, someone dies.”

Ava: “If we move now, this repeats forever.”

Jasper chooses:

Save now → future instability

Delay → immediate loss

⚖️ Type B — People vs System

Harper: protect an individual

Ava: protect the structure

Jasper chooses:

One life now → weakens the world

World stability → costs someone personal

🔥 Type C — Risk vs Control

Harper: calculated risk

Ava: controlled slow path

Jasper chooses:

Unpredictable outcomes

Predictable but morally gray path

4️⃣ What Happens If Jasper Ignores Someone

This is non-negotiable.

Ignore Harper:

Physical consequences

Injuries

Deaths that could have been prevented

Ignore Ava:

Delayed consequences

Betrayals

Systems turning hostile

Reputation damage

Ignore BOTH:

Chaos path

Improvised survival

No safety nets later in the game

5️⃣ NEVER Allow This ❌

Harper and Ava teaming up to overrule Jasper

Ava becoming emotional to “win”

Harper suddenly thinking long-term

Jasper deferring responsibility

If that happens, the system breaks.

🧠 2️⃣ PLAYER-CHOICE MAPPING (AVA WARNINGS)

This is where Ava becomes mechanics, not dialogue.

Ava’s Warning System (Invisible to Player)

Each Ava intervention sets hidden flags, not answers.

Example Ava Line:

“This closes doors we might need later.”

Behind the scenes:

❌ Locks 1–2 future routes

⚠️ Raises suspicion meter

📉 Reduces negotiation success later

The player is never told this explicitly.

Ava Warning Levels
🟢 Soft Warning

Early game

Minimal consequence

Player can ignore safely (once)

🟡 Medium Warning

Mid game

Consequences guaranteed, timing unknown

🔴 Hard Warning

Late game

Ignoring triggers major arc shift

Ava will explicitly say:
“I won’t help you undo this later.”

Player Psychology (Important)

Players learn:

Ava isn’t dramatic

When she speaks, it matters

Silence from Ava = neutral outcome

This trains trust without exposition.

🧩 3️⃣ ANTAGONIST FOILS

(Who beats Ava’s planning)

You cannot have only one type of enemy.

🪞 Foil Type 1 — The Irrational Actor

Beats Ava by:

Acting emotionally

Choosing self-destruction

Ignoring logic entirely

Ava cannot predict them reliably.

This frustrates her more than danger.

🧠 Foil Type 2 — The Meta-Planner

Someone who:

Plans around predictions

Sets traps for likely futures

Exploits Ava’s caution

They punish over-planning.

🔥 Foil Type 3 — The Chaos Catalyst

Not evil — destabilising.

They:

Trigger chain reactions

Force snap decisions

Turn long-term thinking into a liability

Harper thrives here
Ava struggles hard

💀 Foil Type 4 — The Moral Trap

An antagonist who:

Presents “perfect” outcomes

Solves problems efficiently

Does immoral things cleanly

Ava understands them.
That’s the problem.
